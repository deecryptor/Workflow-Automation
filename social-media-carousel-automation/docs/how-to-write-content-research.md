# How to write Content Research

Write one item per line, each starting with a label and a colon:

```
Topic: Insulin resistance
Audience: Working adults 25–55 who think they're healthy because their check-ups are normal. No medical background.
Post I want: An informative post on what insulin resistance is, why it matters and how to reverse it.
Suggested outline: Role of insulin, What is insulin resistance, Health conditions it leads to, Signs and symptoms, Surprising facts, How to reverse it, Call-to-action to save and share
Optional pool (not must-cover): statistics, named experts
```

## What each line does

- **Topic:** the subject the searches are built around. The row's Title is used too.
- **Audience:** shapes the words, examples and "how people talk" research.
- **Post I want:** the goal of the post. It's followed as an instruction.
- **Suggested outline** (or **Outline**) turns on section-by-section mode:
  - **Research:** at least one web search per section, looking for how and why it happens, specific conditions and causes, guideline numbers and expert concepts.
  - **Summary:** a "Your outline" section with 4–7 key points under each of your headings. Gaps are flagged as "Not covered by the sources".
  - **Plan and draft text:** they follow your sections in order, one or two slides each, with a call-to-action slide last.
  - **Format it this way:** keep the outline on one line and separate the sections with commas. It ends at the next line that starts with "Label:".
- **Optional pool:** extras to include when the sources have them, such as statistics or named experts. They're not required.

## Optional extras (each on its own line)

- **Number of slides:** "I want 10 slides" or "Slides: 10". It's kept exactly, up to 20. A number in the **Slide count** field takes priority.
- **Short copy:** "quick tips", "short and punchy", "bite-sized" or "one-liners" switches to short slides. Without one of these, slides are dense and educational: keyword titles and 3–5 full bullets with mechanisms, numbers and plain-language definitions.

## Elsewhere on the row

- **Angle:** write your own hook or angle here *before* Brainstorm to aim the research at it. If it still holds the last suggestion, it's ignored and replaced.
- **Reference Links / Reference Files:** articles, YouTube and Instagram/TikTok/X links, PDFs, videos, images, audio, or .txt transcripts. They're read and summarised into the research. Save Word and PowerPoint files as PDF first.
- **Style Direction:** voice and look. It doesn't steer the research, except that a slide number or short-copy phrase written there also counts.

## Tips

- Name sections the way people search: "Signs and symptoms of…", not "Signs". Your wording becomes the headings and slide titles.
- The facts come only from the research and your references. If a section needs specific details, add a reference link or file that covers them.
